The IRS published its tax year 2027 inflation adjustments on Nov. 5 in Revenue Procedure 2026-35. They include the age-based caps on how much of a premium for a tax-qualified long-term care insurance policy can count as a medical expense. As summarized by the American Association for Long-Term Care Insurance, the cap for people over 70 rises to $6,290 from $6,200 in 2026, and the cap for ages 61 to 70 rises to $5,030 from $4,960. The IRS also raised the daily limit on certain tax-free long-term care benefits to $445 from $430. The premiums count only within the usual rules for itemized medical-expense deductions. For questions about a specific situation, talk to a benefits counselor or an elder law attorney.

Why it matters: Families of someone over 70 who holds a tax-qualified long-term care policy may see a slightly higher limit on deductible premiums for tax year 2027.
