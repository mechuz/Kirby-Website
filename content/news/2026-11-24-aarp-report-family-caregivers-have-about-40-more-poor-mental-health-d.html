A new report from AARP and the National Alliance for Caregiving, part of the Caregiving in the US research series, looks at the health of people who care for relatives and friends. Family caregivers report roughly 40% more days of poor mental health than the average U.S. adult. Money pressure makes a large difference: among caregivers under high financial strain, about three in four report high emotional stress, compared with about three in ten other caregivers, and they are about twice as likely to rate their own health as fair or poor. The findings put a spotlight on caregivers' own well-being as part of planning a loved one's care.

Why it matters: A family caregiver's own health and stress belong in the decision about when to bring in more support.
