A USC-led study published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences used a 1972 British policy that raised the school-leaving age as a natural experiment. People who stayed in school longer because of that change were less likely to be diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease and related dementias decades later. The researchers report that the benefit held even among people with a high genetic risk of the disease.

Why it matters: It adds to research on dementia risk factors that can change over a lifetime; a doctor can talk through brain health with your parent.
