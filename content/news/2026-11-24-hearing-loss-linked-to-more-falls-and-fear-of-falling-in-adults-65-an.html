A study in the Journal of the American Geriatrics Society drew on national survey data from 4,745 Medicare beneficiaries age 65 and older, collected between 2011 and 2022. Both hearing loss measured by testing and hearing trouble people noticed in daily life were associated with falls and with fear of falling. Among those with everyday hearing difficulty, about 40% reported a fall in the prior year, and a similar share said they were afraid of falling. Hearing aid use was not tied to lower odds of either outcome. Because the study shows an association, it cannot prove that hearing loss causes falls.

Why it matters: Changes in a parent's hearing may be worth raising with their doctor as part of a broader talk about fall risk.
