Senior Housing News reports that the incoming Congress is likely to give senior living more attention. Lawmakers have signaled interest in how communities are owned and financed, including private equity and real estate investment structures, and in staffing and workforce practices. At the same time, industry groups see openings to work on affordability for middle-income older adults. Assisted living is regulated mainly by the states, and the industry's trade group says it plans to explain to lawmakers how assisted living differs from nursing homes.

Why it matters: Families comparing communities can expect more public attention on who owns a community and how it is staffed, both good questions to ask on any tour.
