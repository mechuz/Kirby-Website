Florida's health care agency has started writing rules for a new memory care specialty license for assisted living communities, created by a 2026 state law. The draft would set minimum direct-care staffing levels for memory care residents, require licensed nurses to give their medications, and require a community that advertises memory care to hold the license. Provider groups told McKnight's Senior Living they have concerns about parts of the draft, and the state held a public workshop in mid-November. The rules are not final and could change.

Why it matters: States are starting to set their own standards for memory care, so families comparing communities can ask how staffing, training and medication care are handled.
