A new data brief from the CDC's National Center for Health Statistics, drawn from its 2025 national survey of residential care communities (the federal category that includes assisted living), found that 48% of communities had current residents living with each of four common chronic conditions: Alzheimer's disease or another dementia, heart disease, diabetes and depression. About 19% more reported three of the four. Communities with 11 or more beds, those outside metro areas and those in the Northeast and Midwest were more likely to report all four. Communities that did were also more likely to report resident emergency department visits, overnight hospital stays and falls.

Why it matters: Many assisted living residents live with several chronic conditions at once, so it helps to ask any community how it supports dementia, diabetes, heart and mood-related care needs.
