A new AARP report released November 4, "The Hidden Cost of Caregiving," estimates how cutting back on paid work to care for a relative affects a family caregiver's finances over a lifetime. Using an Urban Institute economic model, researchers put the average loss for a working caregiver at about $120,500 in today's dollars, mostly in lost wages, with the rest in reduced workplace retirement income and smaller Social Security benefits. The estimates are modeled for caregivers born in the early 1980s. Caregivers who give up more than 5,000 work hours face far larger losses, very few working caregivers in the analysis kept their full hours, and AARP puts the national total at roughly $2.3 trillion.

Why it matters: Adult children balancing a job and a parent's care often feel the cost years later; this report puts numbers on that tradeoff and can help start family conversations about sharing the load.
