If you are reading this late at night with a calculator open, you are not alone. One of the first questions families ask when a parent needs more help is simple: what will assisted living actually cost in Memphis? The honest answer is that it depends on the apartment, the care your parent needs and what each community includes. But there are solid numbers to start from, and a handful of questions that will help you compare communities fairly.

This guide walks through the latest published cost data for Memphis and Tennessee, what drives the monthly price up or down, how assisted living compares with other kinds of care, and the ways families typically put the pieces together.

This article is general information, not financial, legal or tax advice.

The short answer: what the surveys say

The most widely cited source for senior care prices is the annual Cost of Care Survey, now published by CareScout (formerly the Genworth survey). The most recent edition, the CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025, collected private-pay rates from providers between July and November 2025. Here is what it reported for assisted living:

  • National median: about $6,200 a month, or roughly $74,400 a year, up about 5% from the year before (CareScout release, 2026).
  • Tennessee median: about $5,845 a month (CareScout 2025 state table).
  • Memphis area median: about $5,250 a month, as reported from the same survey by U.S. News & World Report (accessed November 2026).

Two things are worth keeping in mind. First, a median is the middle of the range, not a typical bill: half of communities charge more and half charge less. Second, these are base rates. Your parent's actual monthly cost will depend on the apartment you choose and the level of care they need, which we cover below.

How assisted living compares with other care options

Families often weigh assisted living against hiring help at home or using an adult day program. The same CareScout survey gives national medians for those, too (CareScout, 2025 survey):

  • In-home help (homemaker or home health aide): about $35 an hour. At 44 hours a week, that works out to roughly $80,000 a year, and it does not include rent, utilities, groceries or overnight coverage.
  • Adult day programs: about $95 a day, or around $24,700 a year at five days a week. This can be a good fit while a family caregiver is at work, but evenings, nights and weekends still fall to the family.
  • Nursing home (private room): about $355 a day, or around $129,000 a year. Nursing homes provide skilled medical care around the clock, which is a different level of care from assisted living.

The comparison that matters most is your parent's real day. If they need a few hours of help a week, home care may cost less. Once they need help several times a day, or someone nearby at night, the total cost of staying home (care hours plus the house, food, utilities and transportation) often comes close to, or passes, the cost of assisted living, where meals, housekeeping, activities and staff on site are bundled together.

What drives the monthly price up or down

The apartment

Most communities price by apartment type. A studio usually costs less than a one-bedroom, and a larger or corner apartment can cost more. If a couple moves in together, ask how the second-person fee works.

The level of care

This is the biggest variable. Many communities start with a base monthly rate and then add a care fee based on an assessment of how much help a resident needs with things like bathing, dressing, medications and getting around. Ask how the assessment works, who does it, and how often it is repeated, because care needs and costs can change over time.

Memory care

Memory care for people living with Alzheimer's or another dementia usually costs more than traditional assisted living, because it involves a secure setting, more staff time and specialized programming. CareScout does not publish a separate national median for memory care, so be cautious with any single number you see online and compare actual quotes instead.

One-time fees

Some communities charge a one-time community or move-in fee. Ask whether there is one, what it covers, and whether any part is refundable.

What is included

Two communities with similar monthly rates can be very different values. Ask exactly what the monthly rate covers.

  • Meals: how many a day, and are snacks included?
  • Housekeeping and personal laundry: how often?
  • Utilities, cable and internet
  • Medication management: included or a separate fee?
  • Scheduled transportation to appointments
  • Activities and outings
  • Emergency call system and staff available around the clock
  • How and when rates increase, and how much notice you will get

Questions to ask on every pricing conversation

  1. What is the base monthly rate for the apartment we are considering?
  2. How is the care fee set, and what would it likely be for my parent today?
  3. What happens to the cost if care needs increase? Can my parent stay?
  4. Are there any one-time fees?
  5. What is not included that families usually end up paying for?
  6. How often have rates gone up in recent years?
  7. Is there a minimum stay or notice period if plans change?

Write the answers down for each community you visit. It is the easiest way to compare the real monthly cost, not just the advertised number.

How families usually pay for assisted living

Most families pay for assisted living privately, often by combining several sources. Every family's situation is different, so it is worth talking with a financial planner, an elder law attorney or a benefits counselor before you decide.

Income and savings

Social Security, pensions, retirement accounts and savings are the starting point for most families. Some adult children contribute as well.

The family home

Selling a parent's house, or renting it out, is one of the most common ways families fund a move. Our guide to paying for senior living covers home sales, bridge options and the tax rules to know.

Long-term care insurance

If your parent bought a long-term care insurance policy years ago, it may reimburse some assisted living costs once they meet the policy's requirements. Each policy has its own rules, waiting periods and daily limits, so call the insurer and read the policy carefully.

Veterans benefits

Eligible wartime veterans and surviving spouses who receive a VA pension may qualify for an added monthly amount called Aid and Attendance (VA.gov). The benefit is paid to the veteran or survivor, and the rules on service, income and assets are detailed. A VA-accredited representative or benefits counselor can help you confirm eligibility.

What Medicare and Medicaid do and do not cover

Medicare does not pay for long-term custodial care or the room-and-board cost of assisted living, though it continues to cover eligible medical care a person receives while living there (National Council on Aging). Medicaid programs, including TennCare in Tennessee, have their own long-term care rules, eligibility limits and waiting lists. Please confirm any program questions directly with a benefits counselor before making plans.
