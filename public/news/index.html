Alzheimer's Association launches effort to bring earlier dementia detection into everyday primary care

Alzheimer's Association · Published October 27, 2026 · Shared November 4, 2026

The Alzheimer's Association has launched a national Brain Health and Quality Care Initiative to help health systems build brain health, early detection, diagnosis and dementia care into routine medical care, starting in primary care practices. The goal is for more people to have memory concerns checked and diagnosed earlier, closer to home, rather than waiting for a specialist. The effort builds on the Association's guidance for health systems on running dementia care programs.

Why it matters: If a parent's memory is changing, the family doctor is a good first stop; earlier diagnosis gives a family more time to plan.

Health & research

Study looks at why some brains with Alzheimer's changes stay sharp

Newsweek · Shared November 3, 2026

Some people have the telltale brain changes of Alzheimer's disease yet never develop dementia. A study in the journal Cell Stem Cell, reported by Newsweek, examined donated brain tissue and found a group of immature neurons that may be linked to that resilience. The researchers see it as a clue to why thinking stays intact for some people, and as a direction for future research rather than a treatment.

Why it matters: Early research, but a hopeful sign; a doctor is the best person to talk with about a parent's memory changes.

Read the full story at Newsweek

Senior housing

Senate report calls for tighter nursing home oversight; assisted living left to the states

McKnight's Senior Living · Shared November 3, 2026

A new report from Democrats on the Senate Finance Committee calls for closer federal oversight of nursing homes, including more transparency about who owns them and stricter staffing standards. Senior living groups are watching because the push could spread to other parts of long-term care. The assisted living trade group Argentum noted that the report does not recommend new federal requirements for assisted living, which continues to be licensed and inspected by each state.

Why it matters: Assisted living rules come from the state, so families comparing communities can ask to see the most recent state inspection report.

Social Security benefits will rise 3.6% in January 2027

Social Security Administration · Published October 14, 2026 · Shared October 30, 2026

The Social Security Administration has set a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment for 2027, affecting about 76 million people. Higher Social Security payments begin in January 2027, and increased Supplemental Security Income payments start on December 31, 2026. SSA estimates the average retired worker's monthly benefit will rise by about $75, from $2,095 to $2,170. The adjustment is based on the change in consumer prices measured from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026. Beneficiaries can see their new amount in their my Social Security account in late November, with mailed notices following in December. The 2027 Medicare Part B premium, usually deducted from the same check, will be announced separately by CMS.

Why it matters: If a parent's monthly budget for care depends on Social Security, this is the figure to plan with for 2027.

Read the full story at Social Security Administration

Caregiving & family

Scammers are using the 2027 Social Security raise to target older adults, inspector general warns

Social Security Administration Office of the Inspector General · Published October 21, 2026 · Shared October 30, 2026

Social Security's inspector general warns that scammers are using news of the 3.6% cost-of-living increase to contact beneficiaries. The raise is automatic: nobody needs to register, apply, pay a fee or change their direct deposit to receive it. Scammers posing as Social Security or other agencies may ask for money, personal information or a change of bank account, or send links and software downloads. The inspector general says anyone who suspects a scam should hang up, avoid clicking links, check their own account directly at ssa.gov/myaccount, and report it at ssa.gov/scam or the fraud hotline, 1-800-269-0271.

Why it matters: A good reminder to share with a parent this season: the COLA raise never requires a call, fee or account change.

Read the full story at Social Security Administration Office of the Inspector General

Senior housing

Senior housing occupancy nears a record high as most major markets pass 90%

NIC (National Investment Center for Seniors Housing & Care) · Published October 1, 2026 · Shared October 29, 2026

New third-quarter data from NIC MAP shows senior housing occupancy across the largest U.S. markets rose to about 90.4%, close to a record. Assisted living saw the biggest gain of any housing type, climbing to about 89.1%, while independent living reached about 91.7%. More than half of the primary markets NIC tracks are now above 90% full. At the same time, new construction remains near historic lows, so the supply of new apartments is growing very slowly.

Why it matters: With communities filling up and little new construction, families who start looking early tend to have more choices.

Paying for care

Family caregivers' out-of-pocket costs up about 60% in five years, AARP study finds

NPR · Published October 28, 2026 · Shared October 29, 2026

New AARP research reported by NPR finds that family caregivers now spend an average of about $11,508 a year of their own money on caregiving, roughly 60% more than five years ago. That amounts to around 31% of a typical caregiver's income, and the share is far higher for lower-income households. The story frames the cost of caregiving as rising even faster than rent or gas, putting pressure on family budgets and retirement savings.

Why it matters: Caring for a parent at home has real costs that are easy to underestimate; a benefits counselor can help a family look at the full picture.

Assisted living costs rose about 5 percent in 2025, CareScout survey finds

CareScout · Published March 2, 2026 · Shared October 28, 2026

CareScout's annual Cost of Care Survey, drawn from roughly 16,000 responses from long-term care providers across the country, found that the national median cost of an assisted living community rose about 5 percent in 2025. Home care and nursing home rates rose as well, and a private nursing home room still runs roughly three-quarters more than assisted living. The report breaks the medians down by state and by type of care.

Why it matters: rates vary a great deal from one community to the next, so ask exactly what a monthly rate includes before you compare.

Health & research

More years of school linked to lower dementia risk decades later, study finds

USC Schaeffer Center · Published October 23, 2026 · Shared October 28, 2026

A USC-led study published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences used a 1972 British policy that raised the school-leaving age as a natural experiment. People who stayed in school longer because of that change were less likely to be diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease and related dementias decades later. The researchers report that the benefit held even among people with a high genetic risk of the disease.

Why it matters: It adds to research on dementia risk factors that can change over a lifetime; a doctor can talk through brain health with your parent.

Policy & benefits

Medicare open enrollment is underway: what changes in 2027

PBS NewsHour · Shared October 28, 2026

Medicare's yearly open enrollment runs from October 15 through December 7, 2026, and any change made in that window takes effect on January 1. Among the shifts arriving in 2027 are a higher yearly cap on out-of-pocket drug costs in Part D, the end of a temporary program that had held down some standalone drug plan premiums, and new negotiated prices for 15 more high-cost medicines. Some Medicare Advantage plans are also leaving certain areas or trimming benefits, so the plan's annual notice of change is worth reading closely.

Why it matters: If a parent's plan or medicines are changing, a free Medicare counselor (SHIP) can help compare options before December 7.

Read the full story at PBS NewsHour
