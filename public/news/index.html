Stanford study: resting brain activity may signal Alzheimer's risk years before memory problems

Stanford Medicine · Published November 4, 2026 · Shared November 10, 2026

Stanford Medicine researchers compared brain scans taken at rest from 180 older adults whose memory has stayed exceptionally sharp, often called super agers, and 110 people with Alzheimer's disease. From the differences, they built a score that told the two groups apart about 85% of the time. When the score was applied to more than 1,300 adults aged 55 to 70 with no thinking problems, people whose brain activity looked more like the Alzheimer's group were more likely to show later decline and an Alzheimer's diagnosis. The study appears in Science Advances. The researchers say the method is not ready for use in a doctor's office and that one score does not decide anyone's future.

Why it matters: Research like this may one day help doctors spot risk earlier; families with memory concerns should talk to their doctor.

Paying for care

NPR: The cost of caring for an aging relative is climbing, and Medicare covers little of it

NPR · Published November 3, 2026 · Shared November 10, 2026

In an NPR conversation, a journalist who covers aging explains why the cost of caring for an older relative so often catches families by surprise. Drawing on recent AARP research showing that family caregivers now spend roughly 60% more of their own money than they did five years ago, the segment notes that Medicare pays for very little long-term care. Apart from short stays after a hospitalization, it does not cover everyday help with bathing, dressing or meals. The discussion also points out that typical retirement savings fall well short of what years of paid care can add up to.

Why it matters: Knowing early what Medicare will and won't pay for gives families time to plan; a benefits counselor or elder law attorney can help sort out the options.

Minnesota's assisted living report card shows how residents and families judge quality

McKnight's Senior Living · Shared November 9, 2026

McKnight's Senior Living reports on an industry session that reviewed how Minnesota and Oregon are working to measure assisted living quality. Minnesota's Assisted Living Report Card, modeled on the state's nursing home report card, rates licensed communities using resident quality-of-life and family satisfaction surveys along with state inspection findings on safety and staffing. People consulted in building the tool ranked quality of life, staff quality and resident safety as the most important areas. In the survey results, family members were less satisfied overall than residents, and food was the lowest-rated area for both groups.

Why it matters: Quality of life, staffing, safety and food are worth asking about on any assisted living tour, in Tennessee or anywhere else.

The Pointe at Kirby Gate · Published November 8, 2026 · Shared November 9, 2026

Our new guide answers a question many families ask during Medicare open enrollment. Medicare does not pay for assisted living, because room, meals and daily personal care are not covered. It also lists questions to ask before the December 7 enrollment deadline and points families to Tennessee's free, unbiased SHIP Medicare counseling.

Why it matters: Knowing what Medicare does and does not cover helps families plan before a hospital discharge forces a decision.

Alzheimer's Association: planning holiday gatherings around a loved one with dementia

Alzheimer's Association · Shared November 9, 2026

With Thanksgiving approaching, the Alzheimer's Association's holiday guidance suggests planning ahead and keeping celebrations simple when a family member is living with dementia. Ideas include talking with relatives beforehand about changes in memory and behavior, holding the meal earlier in the day for people who tend to become confused or restless in the evening, and inviting the person to help with familiar, manageable tasks such as setting the table. The Association notes that smaller gatherings may work better, that no caregiver has to keep every tradition, and that relatives can help by giving the primary caregiver even an hour of relief.

Why it matters: A little planning can make Thanksgiving calmer for a parent with dementia and less exhausting for the family caregiver.

Survey: families use AI to build senior living shortlists, but tours and staff still decide

U.S. News & World Report · Published November 4, 2026 · Shared November 8, 2026

A U.S. News & World Report survey of 502 U.S. adults who were researching senior living, or had recently moved a relative or themselves into a community, found that about 70% used generative AI tools during the search, most often to compare communities on a shortlist. Why it matters: AI tools can speed up a search, but care levels and availability are worth confirming directly with each community, in person.

Paying for care

Medicare drug costs in 2027: the yearly out-of-pocket cap rises to $2,400

Kiplinger · Shared November 7, 2026

Kiplinger outlines what changes for people with Medicare prescription drug coverage next year. The annual limit on what a person pays out of pocket for covered Part D drugs goes up from $2,100 in 2026 to $2,400 in 2027, and the maximum plan deductible rises to $700. The deductible counts toward the cap rather than being added on top of it. Average premiums for standalone drug plans are projected to stay close to this year's level. Open enrollment runs through December 7, so plan choices made now take effect January 1.

Why it matters: If a parent takes several costly medications, review their drug plan during open enrollment so the new cap and deductible don't come as a surprise.

Study: Medicare Advantage enrollees less likely to reach five-star nursing homes after a hospital stay

Skilled Nursing News (Health Affairs study) · Published November 3, 2026 · Shared November 6, 2026

A study in Health Affairs looked at more than 1.45 million Medicare beneficiaries discharged from a hospital to a skilled nursing facility in 2022. People in Medicare Advantage plans with narrow provider networks were 22% less likely than those in traditional Medicare to go to a five-star facility, and people in broader networks were 13% less likely. Narrow-network enrollees were also less likely to go to the facility closest to home. The researchers say federal network-adequacy rules should be strengthened.

Why it matters: If a parent has a Medicare Advantage plan, its network can shape which rehab options are offered after a hospital stay; a benefits counselor can explain how the plan works.

Social Security benefits will rise 3.6% in January 2027

Social Security Administration · Published October 14, 2026 · Shared October 30, 2026

The Social Security Administration has set a 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment for 2027, affecting about 76 million people. Higher Social Security payments begin in January 2027, and increased Supplemental Security Income payments start on December 31, 2026. SSA estimates the average retired worker's monthly benefit will rise by about $75, from $2,095 to $2,170. The adjustment is based on the change in consumer prices measured from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026. Beneficiaries can see their new amount in their my Social Security account in late November, with mailed notices following in December. The 2027 Medicare Part B premium, usually deducted from the same check, will be announced separately by CMS.

Why it matters: If a parent's monthly budget for care depends on Social Security, this is the figure to plan with for 2027.

Scammers are using the 2027 Social Security raise to target older adults, inspector general warns

Social Security Administration Office of the Inspector General · Published October 21, 2026 · Shared October 30, 2026

Social Security's inspector general warns that scammers are using news of the 3.6% cost-of-living increase to contact beneficiaries. The raise is automatic: nobody needs to register, apply, pay a fee or change their direct deposit to receive it. Scammers posing as Social Security or other agencies may ask for money, personal information or a change of bank account, or send links and software downloads. The inspector general says anyone who suspects a scam should hang up, avoid clicking links, check their own account directly at ssa.gov/myaccount, and report it at ssa.gov/scam or the fraud hotline, 1-800-269-0271.

Why it matters: A good reminder to share with a parent this season: the COLA raise never requires a call, fee or account change.

The Pointe at Kirby Gate is committed to the letter and spirit of the federal Fair Housing Act and the Tennessee Human Rights Act.
